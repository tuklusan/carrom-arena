## D1 — Board is cut off at the bottom of the 1000×600 window

The current 1000×600 cap in `src/render/renderer.c` clamps the WINDOW correctly but the interior layout still lays out a 484-px square game surface + title + copyright + margins that sums past 600 vertical pixels once system chrome is accounted for. Result: the bottom of the board (including the front cushion + bottom pockets) is chopped.

Fix:
- Recompute `MAX_GAME_SURFACE` from the ACTUAL available height AFTER placing title bar (56 px) + copyright bar (40 px) + top/bottom margins (10 px each) + a **safety reserve of 20 px** for window chrome quirks. That is `600 − 56 − 40 − 20 − 20 = 464` at most.
- Then take `min(that, width_available) = min(464, 960) = 464`. So `MAX_GAME_SURFACE = 460` (a round number ≤ 464).
- Center the game surface both horizontally AND vertically in the remaining space, not just anchored to the top.
- Verify with a fresh Xvfb capture: `identify` the PNG, and inspect visually that the bottom cushion + both bottom pockets are visible with at least 10 px of clearance below them.

## D2 — Motion is way too fast

Two coupled changes.

**Code (`src/app/app.c` capture/rendered loop, `src/common/types.h` or a new `src/common/speed.h`):**
- Add a `playback_speed` field (float, default **0.5**) that scales the *simulation time per rendered frame*. Concretely: in the fixed-step physics loop, do `substeps_this_frame = accumulator / (PHYSICS_DT / playback_speed)` OR advance the accumulator by `dt * playback_speed` instead of `dt`. The important property is: at `playback_speed = 0.5`, one wall-clock second advances 0.5 s of simulation, so pieces visibly move at half real speed. **This must NOT reduce render frame rate** — 60 FPS render, halved physics advance per frame.
- Do NOT change the deterministic seed / RNG usage — only the wall-time-to-sim-time mapping. The seed → outcome invariant must hold at any playback_speed.
- Do NOT slow down `--mode=soak` — that stays real-time-max, `playback_speed = 1.0` default there.

**CLI:**
- New flag `--playback-speed=X` (float, 0.05–4.0). Default 0.5 in `rendered` and `capture` modes; default 1.0 in `soak` and `diagnostic`.
- Existing `+`/`-` keys in rendered mode already change speed — wire them to nudge `playback_speed` up/down by ×2 and /2 respectively, clamped to the same [0.05, 4.0] range. Space still pauses (playback_speed=0 temporarily).

**Update HUD** to show current playback speed (e.g. `Speed: 0.50x`).

## D3 — Baseline lines around the board are confusing

Currently `board_view.c` draws the four baseline rectangles (players' launch zones) as bare colored lines/rects. Operator can't tell what they are.

Replace each baseline visualisation with:
- A **stylised human figure** icon (head circle + shoulders arc + hint of torso), rendered in the seat's team colour (white team = light silhouette outlined in dark; black team = dark silhouette outlined in light).

Do NOT ship photographic assets — use raylib primitives (`DrawCircle`, `DrawRectangle`, `DrawTriangle`, `DrawLineEx`) to compose the figure. Keep total per-figure draw cost trivial.

## D4 — Striker start-of-turn position is unclear

Add a visible **striker placement phase** to the rendered loop, coming BEFORE the strike animation:

1. When a new turn begins, freeze on the placement pose for at least **1.0 s wall time at 1× playback** (`0.5 s / playback_speed`) with the striker on its selected position along the current seat's baseline.
2. During that hold: draw a **pulsing halo** around the striker (`DrawCircleLines` with radius sinusoidally varying, or 3 concentric rings fading out) to unambiguously say "here is where the striker starts this turn".
3. Print a brief HUD banner "Striker placed at (x, y) — striking in Ns" that counts down.
4. After the hold, launch the strike as today.

This gives the human observer time to see the placement before the strike happens.

## D5 — CPU pegs at 100 %, host locks

Two culprits, both must be addressed:

**Render loop:**
- `SetTargetFPS(60)` should already be present. Confirm and add an OS-level throttle: at the end of each rendered frame, if the frame took less than 16.67 ms, `WaitTime` (or a portable `usleep`/`Sleep`) for the remainder. Raylib's `SetTargetFPS` should do this already, but verify it actually calls the sleep — some builds skip it under `--headless`.
- Under `--headless`, the render loop should EITHER still sleep to cap CPU OR spawn no render thread at all. Pick one — I recommend keeping `SetTargetFPS(60)` even headless so the AI doesn't cook the CPU during capture.

**AI thinking (this is the real killer):**
- `src/ai/shot_evaluator.c` runs a synchronous scratch-simulation for every candidate. Currently it can take multiple seconds of tight-loop CPU.
- Introduce a **hard wall-time budget per AI decision**: default 250 ms wall. When exceeded, return the best candidate seen so far.
- Between candidate evaluations, insert a `platform_yield()` (new helper — `sched_yield()` on POSIX, `SwitchToThread()` on Windows) so the OS can schedule other processes. Even a `sleep(0)` between candidates keeps the host responsive.
- Add `--ai-budget-ms=N` CLI (default 250) so this is tunable.

Verify with `top`/`Task Manager` while `carrom_arena --mode=rendered` runs: `carrom_arena` process should sit around 20–30 % of one core, not 100 % of the whole machine.
